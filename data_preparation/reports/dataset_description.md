# Dataset Description — Synthetic Centrifugal Pump Condition & Maintenance Dataset

## ⚠ Synthetic-data disclosure
Every record in this folder was **generated by `scripts/generate_dataset.py`** (fixed seed 359). No record comes
from a real plant, real pump, real manufacturer or real maintenance system. Manufacturer names are deliberately
anonymised (`Vendor A`–`Vendor D`). Costs are plausible USD figures, not quotations. The data must not be
presented as factory records or used to make claims about real pump reliability.

## Purpose
Demonstrate a complete, reproducible data-preparation workflow (collection → quality assessment → cleaning →
preprocessing → EDA → validation) for the CT-359 Centrifugal Pump KBS, and produce a dataset that can later
support knowledge acquisition and rule validation.

Supporting file: `data/raw/pump_asset_register.csv` — clean nameplate master data per pump (type, manufacturer,
installation date, rated flow, rated differential pressure, nominal suction pressure, rated speed, rated current,
utilisation). Real plants keep such a register; it is the authoritative source for equipment attributes.

## How records were generated (engineering-informed, not random)
1. **Pump fleet.** Each pump draws a type, rated flow, rated differential pressure, rated current and a 2- or
   4-pole speed (≈2950 / ≈1475 rpm) from a range typical for that type.
2. **Operating point.** Normal flow varies around 92 % of rated. Differential pressure follows a simple radial
   pump curve, `Δp = Δp_rated · (1.25 − 0.25·q²)` (head rises toward shut-off as flow falls), and motor current
   follows `I = I_rated · (0.45 + 0.5·q)` (power falls at low flow). Speed reflects load-dependent slip.
3. **Fault episodes.** At each step a fault episode may start (probability 0.21). Its type is drawn from base
   frequencies; **wear faults (bearing, seal, impeller) become more likely with operating hours**. An episode
   lasts 1–4 observations with increasing intensity `s ∈ [0,1]` (Low → Critical), modelling progression from
   first detection to repair. Dry running is short and always High/Critical.
4. **Fault signatures** (added to the normal baseline, scaled by `s`):

| Fault | Main sensor effects | Typical recorded observations |
|---|---|---|
| Bearing Failure | vibration ↑↑, bearing temp ↑↑, noise ↑, slight current ↑ | grinding noise |
| Cavitation | suction pressure ↓↓ (<0.75 bar a), vibration ↑, noise ↑↑, flow/head ↓, pressure fluctuation ↑↑ | crackling noise |
| Mechanical Seal Leakage | seal temperature ↑↑ | visible seal leakage |
| Impeller Damage | flow ↓, head ↓, vibration ↑, current per flow ↑ | occasional grinding |
| Shaft Misalignment | vibration ↑↑, bearing temp ↑, current ↑ | coupling vibration |
| Pump Blockage | flow ↓↓, head ↓, suction may ↓, current ↓ | — |
| Dry Running | flow ≈ 0, head ≈ 0, suction ↓, current ≈ 35–50 %, seal & bearing temp ↑↑ | loss of prime, rapid temperature rise |
| Air Ingress | pressure fluctuation ↑↑, flow/head ↓ | air bubbles, occasional loss of prime |
| Discharge Restriction | flow ↓↓ (10–55 %), head ↑ toward shut-off, current ↓, seal/bearing temp ↑ | rapid temperature rise |
| Motor Fault | motor temp ↑↑, current ↑, speed ↓ | unstable speed |
| Unknown | two random faults blended at low intensity | mixed |

5. **Observed symptoms** are what a technician records: sensor-visible symptoms are logged ~85 % of the time
   (operators miss things), non-instrumented observations with fault-dependent probability, and ~3 % of normal
   records carry a false report.
6. **Diagnosis and maintenance.** The last record of an episode is *Confirmed* (90 %) with a root cause,
   an inspection finding, a maintenance action, a maintenance date (lag depends on priority), downtime and cost
   (lognormal around fault-specific medians, scaled by pump size and severity). Earlier records are *Suspected*.
   Unknown episodes are *Inconclusive* and end with "Further investigation scheduled". A routine preventive
   maintenance event occurs about every 2,000 operating hours. Operating hours are monotonic per pump.
7. **Root causes** (2–4 per fault) are recorded only when known; e.g. Bearing Failure ← lubrication deficiency,
   contaminated lubricant, fatigue, or *overload from shaft misalignment* (a fault → fault causal chain whose
   records also carry a misalignment signature).

during dry running) are **not** errors and were not injected — the cleaning must keep them.

## Known limitations of the synthetic data
- Relationships are simplified (linear fault effects, a generic pump curve, independent noise); real faults
  interact, drift and have transients that are not modelled.
- Each record carries one primary fault; genuinely simultaneous independent faults are not generated (only the
  misalignment → bearing chain and the blended Unknown cases).
- Thresholds, frequencies, costs and downtimes are plausible assumptions, not field statistics.
- Every pattern found in EDA was put there by the generator; finding it again verifies the pipeline, not the
  engineering.
